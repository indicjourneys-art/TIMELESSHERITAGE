Planning a Kedarnath–Badrinath Yatra: What to Know Before You Go

By Arjun Bisht · 31 October 2026 · Timeless Heritage

A Himalayan pilgrimage needs more planning than an ordinary holiday. The route, altitude, weather and physical demands all deserve attention before you set out.

Understand the route and the season

Kedarnath and Badrinath are both in Uttarakhand’s Garhwal Himalaya, but reaching them involves mountain roads, changing weather and significant travel time. The pilgrimage season is limited and can change depending on official decisions and conditions.

Before fixing dates, check current temple opening information, road status, weather advisories and any official registration requirements. Do not rely on old travel schedules.

Prepare for altitude and walking

The route to Kedarnath includes a substantial trek from Gaurikund. Travellers should consider their fitness, any existing health concerns and the time needed to walk at a comfortable pace. Facilities and services can change with weather and local conditions.

Pack layers, rain protection, suitable walking shoes, essential medication and identification. Keep luggage manageable and avoid treating the trek as a race.

Keep the itinerary flexible

Mountain travel is sensitive to road conditions, landslides, traffic and weather. A sensible itinerary includes buffer time and does not depend on every transfer running exactly to schedule.

If you are travelling with older family members or children, discuss the walking requirements and available assistance options in advance. Confirm current arrangements directly with the relevant authorities and service providers.

Travel with respect for the mountains

The Kedarnath–Badrinath route is both a pilgrimage and a journey through a fragile mountain environment. Follow local instructions, use designated paths, carry back waste and respect temple and community guidelines.

Good preparation makes the journey safer and more comfortable. The most useful plan is one that leaves room for changing conditions while keeping the purpose of the pilgrimage at the centre.
